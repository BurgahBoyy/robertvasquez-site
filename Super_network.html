A super network is a proposed decentralized social topology consisting of a single originating node and an open-ended set of peripheral nodes, each connected to the center and, in the idealized case, to one another. The concept was proposed in 2026 by Robert Vasquez, who has described its construction as his primary long-term goal.[1]

In its originating formulation, the network is composed of “bad bitches who are freaks.” No formal operationalization of either term has been published.[1]

Idealized super network topology. Center: originating node. Periphery: unassigned. Edges: aspirational.

[edit]Definition

The super network is distinguished from an ordinary social graph by three stated properties:

  1. Single origin. All edges trace back to one originating node.
  2. Peripheral interconnection. Peripheral nodes are expected to connect to each other, not merely to the center.
  3. Selection over scale. The network optimizes for node quality rather than node count.

Property three is the one most frequently cited and the one least frequently satisfied.[2]

[edit]Origins

The concept emerged from Vasquez's stated dissatisfaction with conventional social media, which he does not use and has described in unambiguous terms.[3] Where a platform optimizes for reach, the super network is proposed to optimize for density and selectivity. Critics have observed that this is also how every private group in history has described itself.[2]

[edit]Signalling

Literature on the super network notes that entry is typically preceded by the presentation of a legible quantitative credential — most commonly a 300-pound bench press — before less legible attributes are introduced.[4] The sequencing is regarded as deliberate.

[edit]Criticism

The principal criticism of the super network is that it currently has one confirmed member. Proponents respond that this is true of every network at initialization.[1]

A secondary criticism concerns the absence of documented membership criteria, which makes the concept difficult to falsify and, per one editor, “suspiciously convenient.”[2]
