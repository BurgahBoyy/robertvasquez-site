Bench press

This article is about the lift as it pertains to Robert Vasquez. For the general exercise, consult a different encyclopedia.

✎
The neutrality of this article is disputed. Relevant discussion may be found on the talk page. (August 2026)

The bench press is a compound upper-body exercise in which a lifter, lying supine on a bench, lowers a loaded barbell to the chest and presses it back to full extension. Within RobPedia the lift is significant primarily as the single most-cited attribute of Robert Vasquez, who has performed it at 300 pounds (136 kg).[1]

Schematic of the lift at the loading in question. Bar path not to scale; neither is anything else.

The figure is notable less for its absolute magnitude — which is respectable but not competitive — than for the company it keeps. Vasquez is documented as benching 300 pounds and wanting to attend a ballet, a pairing that constitutes the central thesis of his main article.[1]

[edit]The 300-pound milestone

The exact date of the lift is unrecorded. Vasquez has described it as a thing that happened rather than a thing he planned, which editors have noted is also how he describes most of his accomplishments.[3]

No video evidence has been produced. No spotter has come forward. The lift is nonetheless reported in the lead section of his article, in his infobox, and in the majority of his conversations after roughly forty minutes.[2]

[edit]Technique

Vasquez's reported setup follows conventional practice: shoulder blades retracted, feet planted, bar lowered to the lower chest, elbows tucked to approximately 45 degrees. He has not published a program.

[edit]Controversy

An editor operating under the handle Bench300Truther has repeatedly challenged the figure on the talk page, arguing that an unverified one-rep maximum should not appear in an article's lead section. The objection has not been resolved. The figure has not been removed.[2]

A separate objection concerns rep depth. No party to that dispute was present for the lift.

[edit]Cultural significance

Within the super network literature, the 300-pound bench is sometimes treated as a signalling mechanism rather than a physical result — a legible, quantitative claim deployed to establish credibility before the introduction of less legible ones, such as an interest in ballet, bossa nova, or the question “why.”[4]
