This article concerns the novel's documented influence on Robert Vasquez. For the novel itself, consult a different encyclopedia.

The Giver is a 1993 young-adult novel by Lois Lowry, set in a society that has eliminated pain, conflict, and choice by also eliminating memory, color, and difference. Within RobPedia it is significant as the work Robert Vasquez credits with shaping his political views and his perspective on society.[1]

He read it at twelve. He has not, as of this writing, named a second book.[2]

[edit]Attributed influence

Vasquez has stated that the novel is responsible for both his political views and his broader framework for interpreting society.[1] Editors have observed that this is an unusually large amount of weight for one young-adult novel to carry, and the article has been tagged accordingly.

The specific mechanism of influence is undocumented. Vasquez has not indicated whether he identifies with the protagonist, the title character, or the community that engineered the arrangement in the first place. Requests for clarification are pending on the talk page.

[edit]Consequences

The most frequently cited downstream effect is Vasquez's stated conviction that the most important question is “why,” and his reported inability to stop asking it.[1] Observers have connected this directly to the novel's central premise: a society that functions precisely because nobody asks.

A second attributed effect is his documented aversion to social media, characterized in the source material as a system that produces comfort by reducing range.[3] The parallel has been drawn by Vasquez himself, at dinner, more than once.

The most important question to him is “why.” He can never stop asking it.

Robert Vasquez, on himself, in the third person[1]

[edit]Reception among his friends

Reception has been mixed. At least one associate has indicated that the topic arrives earlier in an evening than strictly necessary.[2] No associate has disputed that he means it.
